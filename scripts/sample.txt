Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that combines information retrieval with language model generation.
Instead of relying solely on parametric knowledge stored in model weights, RAG fetches relevant documents at query time.
This allows the model to ground its responses in external, up-to-date knowledge sources.

The retrieval step typically involves embedding both the query and documents into a shared vector space.
Nearest neighbor search then identifies the most semantically relevant chunks.
These chunks are passed as context to the generator, which produces a cited, grounded response.

Chunking strategy significantly impacts retrieval quality.
Fixed chunking splits text at regular character intervals regardless of semantic boundaries.
Semantic chunking respects sentence and paragraph boundaries, often improving retrieval precision.
